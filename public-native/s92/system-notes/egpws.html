EGPWS

EGPWS

Five active modes — Mode 1 isn't used on the S-92 — plus Look-Ahead, Peaks Mode terrain coloring, and the geometric altitude behind all of it.

Source: S-92A RFM Part 2, Section I, Chapter 4 (Avionics Management System — Enhanced Ground Proximity Warning System: Introduction, Theory of Operation, Terrain Display, Auto Range and Cell Expansion, Pop Up Mode, Look Ahead Mode, Basic Modes, Controls, EGPWS Test). The S-92 uses EGPWS as its aircraft aural warning generator.

What it is

EGPWS reduces Controlled Flight Into Terrain (CFIT) risk through two complementary alert families: the basic modes (2 through 6), based on radar altitude, which prevent descent into level or evenly sloping terrain; and the Look-Ahead modes, based on GPS position compared against terrain and obstacle databases, which catch sharply rising terrain or man-made obstacles ahead of the flight path.

Note that Mode 1 (excessive descent rate) is not used on the S-92 — active protection starts at Mode 2.

A geometric altitude — barometric altitude, radar altitude, and GPS altitude combined — drives the terrain display and alerting, preventing errors from a wrong altimeter setting, a faulty altimeter, or extreme cold. The obstacle database covers man-made structures over 100 ft high; not all obstacles are covered worldwide, and power lines are generally not included.

Peaks Mode shows two numbers in the lower left of the terrain display: the highest terrain/obstacle in the selected range (colored red/yellow/green per its relationship to aircraft altitude), and the green/black interface altitude — both in feet AMSL.

Look-Ahead mode

Using the predicted flight path vector compared to the terrain/obstacle database, Look-Ahead issues a caution at roughly 30 seconds from impact and escalates to a warning at roughly 20 seconds — both remain active until the aircraft maneuvers clear. Alert timing is slightly reduced in low altitude mode.

Look-Ahead is inhibited when TER INHB is selected or airspeed is below 70 KIAS. If GPS is lost, EGPWS dead-reckons for 60 seconds; if GPS isn't regained, a 'BE ALERT, TERRAIN INOP' aural sounds and the terrain map is removed.

When an alert occurs, the terrain display range automatically changes to 5 NM (2.5 NM in low altitude mode) and the offending terrain area is enlarged on the display (cell expansion) to draw attention to it. If no terrain display is selected anywhere when an alert triggers, the system pops one up automatically — center MFD first, then pilot inboard, then copilot inboard MFD (disabled with two or fewer MFDs operational).

Since EGPWS and weather radar share display colors, the two cannot be selected simultaneously on the same MFD. EGPWS ground test (engines off only) runs a full IBIT sequence across two MFDs and takes about 20 seconds, ending in a TEST PASSED message.
