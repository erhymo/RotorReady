Hydraulic System

Hydraulic System

Three fully independent systems with full cross-redundancy, plus Leak Detection and Isolation — how a single leak gets contained without taking down a second system.

Source: S-92A RFM Part 2, Section I, Chapter 14 (Hydraulic System — Introduction, Hydraulic System Controls, Hydraulic Pressure Indicator, Hydraulic Pump Modules, Heat Exchangers, Transfer Modules, Automatic Backup Shutoff Valve Operation, Leak Detection and Isolation, Main Rotor Servos, Tail Rotor Servos, Operation).

Three independent systems, full redundancy

No. 1 and No. 2 are the primary flight-control hydraulic systems, each fully capable on its own. No. 3 is a genuine backup — it can supply either or both of No. 1/No. 2 if a pump fails, and independently powers the landing gear, cargo ramp, and automatic APU accumulator recharge. All three are completely independent constant-pressure systems (4,000 psi, up to 13 GPM each on No. 1/2); No. 1 and No. 2 pump modules are identical and interchangeable, driven by their respective accessory modules. No. 3 is driven directly off the main gearbox, with a separate reservoir and an AC-motor-driven ground-checkout mode for pre-rotor testing.

A four-channel boost system (collective, pitch, roll, yaw) is powered by No. 2 hydraulics via a BST switch — turning it OFF illuminates BOOST PRES and noticeably increases control forces (cyclic roughly 1 lb → 6 lb per axis; collective/yaw roughly 4 lb → 13 lb), with some collective-to-yaw interaction. SAS hydraulic power comes through shutoff valves on the pilot-assist manifold, controlled by the SAS1/SAS2 switches on the AFCS panel — below 1,100 psi, SAS 1 PRES or SAS 2 PRES illuminates.

The SERVO switch — isolating one side deliberately

A SERVO switch (NO. 1 / BOTH / NO. 2) on the upper console turns off one side of the three primary flight-control servos and the tail rotor servo. Selecting NO. 1 turns off the No. 2 servo side (MR SVO 2 PRES and TR SVO 2 PRES illuminate); selecting NO. 2 turns off the No. 1 side (MR SVO 1 PRES and TR SVO 1 PRES illuminate). An interlock prevents deliberately turning off a servo side while the other side has already lost pressure — and if the side left running loses pressure, the previously-off side automatically re-enables.

Automatic backup shutoff valves

Both backup shutoff valves (which connect No. 3 to No. 1/No. 2) close automatically during engine start and shutdown, to prevent unwanted pressure shuttling between systems. During start, the DCUs close them once the first engine passes 15% Ng, reopening once Nr exceeds 50%. During shutdown, they close once Nr drops below 50%, reopening two minutes after the last engine's Ng drops below 15%.

With the backup shutoff valves closed, No. 3 pressure is unavailable to the primary, SAS, and boost servos — but remains available for the ramp, landing gear, and APU accumulator. During engine start, the pilot cannot move the flight controls until No. 1 and/or No. 2 pressure is established, regardless of No. 3 status; after shutdown, No. 3 can't be used to move the flight controls until the two-minute reopen delay has elapsed.

Leak Detection and Isolation (LDI)

If a No. 1 or No. 2 reservoir level drops below its set limit: the corresponding RSVR LOW caution illuminates, that system's tail rotor shutoff valve closes to isolate a possible leak in the tail rotor circuit, and its backup shutoff valve closes to isolate the leaking primary system from No. 3 — protecting the No. 3 backup capability for the still-healthy system. A subsequent leak in the other primary system does not also trigger those valves; each side is isolated independently as its own leak is detected.

The No. 3 system runs at a lower pressure range in depress mode when idle, switching to the full 3,500–4,200 psi high mode (HYD 3 HI MODE advisory) whenever it's actually being used — landing gear, ramp, or backing up No. 1/2.
