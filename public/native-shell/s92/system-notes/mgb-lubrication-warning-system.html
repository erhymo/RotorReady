MGB Lubrication and Warning System

MGB Lubrication and Warning System

The main gearbox's own layered defense — pressure alert, oil-hot caution, five chip detectors, and the bearing monitor system, with the exact caution/warning split.

Source: S-92A RFM Part 2, Section I, Chapter 12 (Powertrain — Introduction, Components, MGB Lubrication System, MGB Indicating System, MGB Oil Pressure Alert System, MGB Oil Bypass Switch, MGB Oil Hot Caution, MGB Chip Detection, Bearing Monitor System, Rotor Brake).

What it is

The Main Gearbox (MGB) sits atop the cabin forward of the two engines, built from five modules — two input modules, two accessory modules, and the main module — with a built-in 5° forward tilt. Dual pumps mounted on the front of the MGB supply cooled, filtered oil to all bearings and gears; an external radiator cooled by a blower on the tail rotor drive shaft prevents overheating, with a bypass valve able to isolate the cooler entirely. The oil filter has an impending-bypass button that should be checked on preflight for contamination.

The oil temperature and pressure gauges live in the EICAS MGB group (PSI / °C), fed independently through each DCU — the border, digital readout, and tape turn yellow if either value moves outside normal range, and red if it reaches a never-exceed value.

Oil pressure alert — a separate system from the gauge

Independent of the cockpit pressure gauge, a dedicated alert system monitors oil pressure at the input manifold and at the 'last jet' of the left accessory module via two pressure switches that close when pressure drops below minimum. If either switch trips: MGB OIL PRES caution. If both trip together: MGB OIL PRES warning, with a 'GEARBOX PRESSURE' aural alert. This dual-switch design is the difference between a single-point sensor fault and a genuine dual-indication pressure loss.

MGB OIL HOT

Each chip detector doubles as a temperature sensor — high oil temperature triggers the MGB OIL HOT caution and, crucially, also disables the chip detector's fuzz burn-off feature, to prevent electrical arcing while the oil is in a vaporous state near that temperature. Power for this caution comes from the DC ESSENTIAL bus via the M XMSN OIL WARN breaker — the same circuit as the oil pressure alert system.

Chip detection

Five chip detectors — one each in the MGB, both input modules, and both accessory modules — feed a chip detector processor, generating MGB CHIP, INPUT 1/2 CHIP, and ACC 1/2 CHIP cautions independently. A 30-second time-delay relay lets small chips and fuzz burn off or wash away before triggering a caution, preventing false alarms from normal wear debris — this fuzz burn-off function is what MGB OIL HOT disables. The detector runs a Built-In-Test on startup; a BIT failure shows CHIP SYS FAULT.

Bearing Monitor System (BMS)

The BMS processes vibration and temperature data from the swashplate, tail rotor drive shaft bearings #1 and #2, and the oil cooler bearing. An out-of-limit reading on any of them illuminates CHECK BMS. Swashplate over-temperature specifically can escalate to a warning or caution depending on severity — a separate, more serious condition from the routine BMS advisory.

Rotor brake — a separate hydraulic circuit

The rotor brake pad/disc assembly mounts on the MGB tail rotor output flange, engaged by pulling down and forward on a handle over the pilot's left shoulder — more forward movement or repeated strokes increases brake pressure, shown on an adjacent gauge. The rotor brake reservoir doubles as the servicing reservoir for hydraulic systems 1, 2, and 3.

Both engines can be started with the rotor brake set — the FADEC won't advance either engine past idle until Np exceeds 24%. The rotor brake is not designed to stop the rotor before shutdown; it's a hold/slow-down function, not a rapid-stop function. Pressure and puck-position sensors drive the ROTOR BRAKE caution: on the ground, either sensor alone triggers it; in the air, both sensors must agree to trigger it.
