TCAS: Traffic Alert and Collision Avoidance System

TCAS: Traffic Alert and Collision Avoidance System

Traffic-Advisory-only — no Resolution Advisories on the S-92. The sensitivity levels, symbol set, and pop-up display logic behind every TA.

Source: S-92A RFM Part 2, Section I, Chapter 6 (Navigation — TCAS: Introduction, TCAS Operation, Display Characteristics and Modes, Controls and Functions, Initial Built-in Test).

What it does — and what it doesn't

TCAS on the S-92 issues Traffic Advisories (TAs) only — there is no Resolution Advisory capability. It detects and tracks nearby transponder-equipped aircraft (ATCRBS Mode A/C or Mode S), computing range, relative bearing, closure rate, and — if the intruder reports it — relative altitude and vertical trend, to predict time to and separation at Closest Point of Approach (CPA). A TA fires when time to CPA is 15 to 30 seconds, alerting the crew with a 'Traffic, Traffic' aural and a TRAFFIC annunciation on the ADI.

TCAS traffic can be displayed together with weather radar, EGPWS, and/or lightning sensor data. ATC procedures and see-and-avoid remain the primary means of separation — TCAS is a backup, particularly valuable if communication with ATC is lost.

Range, altitude bands, and sensitivity levels

Nominal maximum range is 18 NM, though intruders can occasionally be seen out to 36 NM — TCAS reduces its tracking volume in high-density areas to limit interference and processing load. It tracks up to 45 aircraft and displays up to 30.

Two sensitivity levels adjust the TA threshold by altitude band, to avoid nuisance alerts in busy low-level traffic: SL A applies below 2,000 ft AGL or with the landing gear extended; SL B applies in all other flight conditions.

LevelTA triggers
SL AAltitude separation closing to <600 ft within 20 s at current rate; OR separation <600 ft altitude AND <0.20 NM range; OR a non-altitude-reporting intruder within 15 s or 0.20 NM
SL BAltitude separation closing to <800 ft within 30 s at current rate; OR separation <800 ft altitude AND <0.55 NM range; OR a non-altitude-reporting intruder within 20 s or 0.55 NM

Display modes and symbols

Three display volumes select how much airspace is shown: Normal (−2,700 to +2,700 ft), Above (−2,700 to +9,000 ft), Below (−9,000 to +2,700 ft).

SymbolMeaning
Open diamondIntruder beyond 1,200 ft relative altitude or 5 NM range — not yet a threat
Filled diamondIntruder within 1,200 ft and 5 NM — proximate traffic, still not considered a threat
Filled yellow circleTraffic Advisory — the intruder is potentially hazardous
An altitude tag in hundreds of feet shows above (+) or below (−) the symbol, with a trend arrow if vertical rate exceeds 500 fpm. If TCAS can't resolve an intruder's bearing, it shows a no-bearing text advisory instead — format TA n.nNM ±aa with a climb/descend arrow — positioned below the aircraft symbol; up to two no-bearing intruders can be shown at once, ranked by priority.

Pop-up display

If no TCAS display is currently selected and more than three MFDs are operational, a traffic warning forces a NAV ARC page with TCAS onto an available display automatically — center MFD first (if installed), then pilot's inboard MFD, then copilot's inboard MFD. EGPWS pop-up takes precedence over TCAS if both trigger together. Pop-up is disabled when only two or fewer MFDs are functional.

Controls and self-test

TCAS is controlled from the PFD ARC and NAV displays: press OVLY then TCAS to bring up the display, CTL to activate TCAS controls, and BAND repeatedly to step through ABV/NORM/BLW. STBY places TCAS in standby — reactivate by pressing OVLY and reselecting TCAS.

Initial Built-In Test (IBIT) runs on the ground via the maintenance data computer: press HEALTH, line down to AVIONICS, SUB STAT, line down to TCAS, then IBIT.
