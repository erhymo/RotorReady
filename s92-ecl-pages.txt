PRE Preamble
FIRE
1 Fly the aircraft and maintain N R
ENG In all emergency / abnormal situations, it is essential that a complete division of
responsibility exists, such that PF continues to ensure the safe operation of the
aircraft and PM deals with the situation.
APU
The immediate and subsequent action of the PF is to FLY THE AIRCRAFT. PM shall
assist PF by monitoring the flight path and the engine and systems instruments.
FUEL
1. MAINTAIN AIRCRAFT CONTROL
PWRTN 2. ANALYSE THE SITUATION
3. TAKE APPROPRIATE ACTION
T/RTR
Before movement of any throttle, fuel control, or critical systems switch, PM will call
for confirmation of selection by PF.
HYD
Once the aircraft is under control and on the command of PF, PM shall identify and
state all the displayed warnings and cautions, identify the malfunction together with
ELEC PF, then cancel the MASTER CAUTION . It is implicit in the drills that PF will
continue to control the aircraft.
CTRL
Once the immediate actions are complete, if the commander is PF, they should
consider handing over the control to the co-pilot in order to better manage the
A/ICE situation.
For all system failures, the drills presume that circuit breakers have been checked.
RIPS
Circuit breakers should not normally be reset. They may be reset once only,
provided there are no indications of an electrical fire and the affected system is
necessary for the safe recovery of the aircraft.
MISC
Drills are preceded by the drill number and title, thus:
HOIST
01 DRILL TITLE
LAND
Indications:
• Caution and warning lights, sounds, faults, and other indications that may occur. To
SUPP
facilitate reading at night, captions that, in the aircraft and in the RFM, appear as red text
on a black background are shown as white text on a red background (e.g.
CAS MGB OIL PRES ).
Actions:
1. Immediate actions, outlined in black, shall be carried out from memory, and
verified from the checklist

2. All required subsequent actions
FIRE
2 Definitions
ENG
LAND / DITCH IMMEDIATELY
The consequences of continued flight are likely to be more hazardous than, for
APU
example, ditching, or landing in trees. Where the instruction 'Land or ditch
immediately' is given in a checklist, the procedure for 'emergency landing' should be
FUEL followed.
LAND AS SOON AS POSSIBLE
PWRTN
Land at the nearest safe place. Depending on the malfunction and where it occurs,
this may require flight to a runway or to the nearest landfall or helideck. The flight
T/RTR
should be at an altitude and airspeed that will allow for an immediate landing or
ditching if the abnormal condition deteriorates.
HYD
LAND AS SOON AS PRACTICAL
Land at the nearest airfield at which technical support can be achieved. If this is not
ELEC
practical, land at a safe landing site chosen for subsequent convenience. The
duration of the flight and landing site are at the discretion of the pilot. Extended
CTRL flight is not recommended.
SAFE OEI FLIGHT CONDITIONS
A/ICE
Safe OEI flight is defined as a sustainable airspeed of not less than V TOSS , the ability
to obtain a positive rate of climb at acceptable power levels, and an altitude which
RIPS provides sufficient clearance from the ground / obstacles so that required
manoeuvring can be reasonably achieved. At crew discretion, procedural
checks / actions may be carried out while these conditions are being established.
MISC
ESTABLISH SAFE FLIGHT CONDITIONS
HOIST
Establish a safe flight path, while continuing with the emergency procedure, to
reduce the time necessary for conduct of a possible emergency landing.
LAND
CONTINUE FLIGHT
If the fault persists, or returns after clearing, the decision to continue as planned or
SUPP
to take alternative action shall consider the implications of further deterioration of
the affected system.
CAS If the fault clears, the flight may continue as planned without restriction.
WARNING
Draws attention to an operating procedure, practice, or condition that may result in
injury or death if not carefully observed or followed.
